Venture Global: Its Customers Own The War, Its Shareholders Own The Glut

I rate Venture Global (VG) a Sell, with moderate conviction and a 24-month price target of $11.25, against the October 7 close of $13.05. The call comes down to one test. To justify $13.05, the LNG market has to be in shortage about 44% of the time after 2032. From 2011 to 2026, a stretch that brought Fukushima, the Russia shock and Hormuz, it was in shortage 43% of the time. So the price assumes the next two decades look like the last fifteen years. I don't think they will, because a supply wave the size of 2015–20's is about to land. In fee terms, every open cargo Venture Global sells from 2032 has to average about $4.49 per MMBtu. No fee reported on a new US contract since 2023, war included, has topped about $3.15.

The next six months should help the stock: I expect a guidance raise on November 9, and my 2027 EPS is well above consensus. This is a call on valuation, not on management. Selling forward is how you finance plants, and Venture Global builds faster than anyone. For readers who want to trade it, the options near the end are small, and I'm explicit about how little edge they carry.

## The Customers Own The War

On October 7, Venture Global reported its third-quarter cargoes: 465.8 TBtu at an implied liquefaction fee of $6.79 per MMBtu. On the company's own formula, JKM less 115% of Henry Hub less $2, a spot cargo over the same quarter would have cleared about $17.43 on my estimate. That's roughly $7.0 billion of spread above Calcasieu's $2.36 contract fee. By my split, Calcasieu's 20-year buyers ended up with about 30% of it, the buyers of Plaquemines' forward-sold cargoes with 41%, and Venture Global with 29%. These are my estimates, not company figures: the company reports one blended fee, so I back Plaquemines' share out of it, and September's JKM average is my estimate. Treat the split as approximate.

[[CHART 2, Datawrapper gYAn7: Who ended up with the Q3 spread (author's estimates)]]

Even Venture Global's uncontracted cargoes earn the price on the day they're signed, not spot. The prospectus warned, and the latest 10-K still says, that those sales "may be uncorrelated with movements in spot LNG prices." By February 25, three days before the war began, 69% of 2026 was already contracted, and the March and August decks make it policy: "We will continue to forward contract additional cargos on a rolling basis." So the futures curve is roughly the price Venture Global signs at, and the thesis turns on what that price does after 2030.

More of the 2030s is about to be exposed, not less. In August, Sabel told analysts that for the industry's slow-build projects, 20-year contracts are "a requirement to make the math work," but that Venture Global's 18-to-20-month builds are "a much different formula," so the bolt-ons will carry "more midterm contracts." The August deck says it outright: 47 of 85 MTPA is on 10-to-20-year contracts, 6 is medium-term and 32 is still available. That's 38% uncontracted today and 45% once the medium-term deals roll off. Each MTPA left open is about $52 million a year for every $1 of fee.

## The Long-Run Fee Depends On The Regime

The bull case leans on Venture Global's favorite slide: an implied US fee from 2010 to 2026 with a mean of $6.41 and a median of $5.19, and short-term fees "2x – 3x greater than the 20-year contract rates." That average mixes two markets. On the IMF's TTF series (and its Asia LNG series from 2017), the fee averaged about $4.78 after Fukushima, about $18.80 in 2021–22 and about $10.80 to $11.90 so far in 2026. In the 2015–2020 supply wave it averaged about $0.45 on the TTF basis and $1.62 on the JKM basis, below the contract fee. The years after Russia, 2023–25, averaged about $6.70 to $7.30.

[[CHART 3, Datawrapper OPSnE: The fee depends on the regime]]

There has been only one prior wave, so this rests on a single example. The case that the 2030s resemble it is about size. World LNG trade grew 45% from 2015 to 2019, from 245 Mt to 355 Mt (IGU). The IEA expects net LNG supply to rise by 250 bcm a year by 2030, about 184 Mt or 42% on top of 2025's 437 Mt. It says the war's 140 bcm of losses are "largely concentrated in 2026 and 2027." Demand is already bending at war prices: the IEA estimates Asian gas demand fell 0.5% in the first half and China's LNG imports 12% from March to June, with spot Asian LNG averaging $17.50 in the second quarter. On October 7 settlements, the implied fee is $15.41 for 2027, $8.03 for 2028, $5.12 for 2029, $3.80 for 2030 and $2.50 for 2031. One caveat matters: CME shows only 175 to 215 contracts of JKM open interest a month for 2027–29 and none for 2030–31, so the last two are settlement marks, not traded prices. The traded evidence for those years is in contracts.

## What Buyers Actually Sign

The cleanest test of $4.49 is what buyers pay for new capacity. I collected every fee reported on new US contracts since late 2023. Poten put 20-year deals at about $2.40 to $2.50 in 2023 and fees under discussion at $2.65 to $2.95 in early 2025. In November 2025, Reuters reported Venture Global charging $2.30 on new long-term contracts, Cheniere over $2.75 and Woodside about $2.90 for 10 years. Argus's indicative long-term price is 115% of Henry Hub plus $3. Shorter tenors pay most: Sabel called one five-year deal "north of a $3 net spread," and three weeks into the war Vitol's five-year contract priced at $2.80 to $3.20 over 119% of Henry Hub, according to traders cited by Platts. On a like-for-like basis the highest is about $3.15. None is within $1.30 of $4.49. Most are long tenors that price a fixed fee rather than a spot spread, but they are the only traded prices for the years the futures can't reach.

## What A New Plant Needs

Replacement cost doesn't cap the fee; in 2021–22 the fee ran far above it. What it does is anchor the average. When the fee averages above what a new plant needs, developers build until it doesn't. The record 2025 FID year is that response.

I took the announced costs of four recent US greenfield projects from the developers' own releases. Woodside's Louisiana LNG is about $964 a tonne, excluding the pipeline. Rio Grande's first phase is about $847 once its $3.1 billion of financing costs come out of the $18 billion headline. Port Arthur's first phase is about $1,000, and Commonwealth about $1,368 on its $13 billion, a figure that may include financing. Cheniere gave no cost for its two new Corpus Christi trains, so I left them out. For each project I solve for the flat fee that earns 8% after tax over a 35-year life (20 contracted years plus 15 at the same fee). The build takes four years, operating cost is Venture Global's own $0.92 per MMBtu, and the tax rate is 21% with the 100% bonus depreciation the 2025 tax law restored.

The answer is $2.68 to $3.00 for the first three and $3.76 for Commonwealth. On a 20-year life alone it's $3.01 to $3.39, and $4.30 for Commonwealth. At 7% the fees fall about $0.25, and without bonus depreciation they rise about $0.17. Sabel argued in March that long-term contract prices need "$3.50 to $4.50 minimum" to support returns, but he premised it on a global replacement cost "north of $2,000 per ton." The Gulf Coast projects above are announcing about half that, and they're the ones competing with Venture Global's open cargoes.

The strongest case for a higher floor comes from the gas exporters' forum, GECF, which puts the levelized cost of proposed North American projects at about $3.80 to $4.00, against about $2.66 for those already under construction. The plants being built now should set the price into the early 2030s; the next tranche only gets built if fees rise toward its cost. That's why my $3.50 sits between the two, a 50-to-80-cent premium over today's replacement cost.

The regimes give the test a bull should accept. From 2011 to September 2026 the market was in shortage 43% of the months, at an average fee of about $9.60, while the 2015–20 wave averaged $0.45. If the years between shortages look like 2015–20, a through-cycle fee of $4.49 needs shortages 44% of the time. If they look like 2015–25 as a whole, 28% would do. My $3.50 assumes about a third. One bias runs in the bull's favor: I measure 2011–14 on TTF, because spot JKM data before 2017 sits behind paywalls, and Asian prices ran higher after Fukushima. Every $1 of understatement cuts the shortage share the price needs by about 2.5 points, to 41% at $1 and 33% at $5. Against that, the shareholder captures only part of a spike: in Q3, Venture Global's $6.79 was 39% of my $17.43 spot estimate, because it sells forward.

## Valuation And Price Target Methodology

I value the parts separately and subtract claims: net debt, the $3.0 billion Series A preferred, Stonepeak's 23% of Calcasieu, and half the low end of BP's claim. The contracted book is a take-or-pay annuity discounted at 7%, which is where Venture Global's secured debt prices today. Calcasieu's April notes came about 1.7 points over the 10-year and the parent's June notes about 1.9 to 2.2. With the 10-year at 5.28%, that's about 7.0% and 7.3%. Open cargoes rank behind the debt and the 9.0% preferred, so they get 10%.

Deals point to the same rates. Sempra's own Port Arthur figures, about $1.64 billion of EBITDA a year on $13 billion for a plant with its long-term capacity fully contracted, imply about a 7.6% unlevered after-tax return over the 20-year contracts (8.9% before tax). Cheniere, "90% or more" contracted, trades at about an 8.5% EBITDA yield on the $8 billion-plus run rate its CFO cites. Contracted LNG cash clears near 7% to 8%; open cargoes should cost more. Infrastructure money draws the same line: Stonepeak's $5.7 billion in Louisiana LNG bought a tolling stream, while Woodside kept cost overruns, gas supply and offtake.

Three inputs come from the company. Contract fees escalate: its definition of contracted revenue assumes "approximately 17.5% of the fixed facility charge component increases by 2.5% annual inflation," worth about $0.87 a share. Its $138 billion of total contracted third-party revenue implies an average base fixed fee of $2.09 to $2.67, depending on what it counts in its "net" lifting charge. I use $2.45 plus $0.40 for CP2. Costs calibrate to about $1.15 per MMBtu in 2026, including about $435 million of Plaquemines basis cost that the company says declines in 2028. Without it the cost is about $0.92, which I allow from 2029; that's worth about $3.20 a share. Depreciation comes from the balance sheet: $1.0 billion a year on $37.7 billion of plant in service.

On that basis Venture Global is worth about $9.00 a share today and $11.27 at the end of 2028, which sets the $11.25 target. Per-share values use the 2.643 billion diluted shares in the 10-Q, which include 154 million dilutive stock options. Without that dilution, they'd be $9.51 and $11.92. Each $1 of long-run fee is worth about $4.10 a share. A lasting $1 rise in Henry Hub takes about $4.70 off, and since the export wave is a large new source of US gas demand, the two risks sit on the same side.

The rating rests on a second judgment: that open cargoes deserve 10%. CAPM would put the equity's cost at about 9.1% (beta 0.92 on daily returns since the IPO, Damodaran's 4.20% implied premium), but that beta explains only about 3% of the stock's daily moves, and on weekly returns it's 0.40, so I give it little weight. At CAPM-consistent rates, 6% for contracted cash and 9% for open cargoes, the base case is worth $12.61 today and the weighted end-2028 value is $15.74, a 23% return over two years, roughly what the market itself should earn. On that lens the stock is fairly priced and Hold would be the rating. I rate it a Sell because the deal evidence above says open commodity exposure costs more than a 3%-explained beta implies, and that's why conviction is moderate.

Multiples look cheap at first glance. On 2026 guidance, Venture Global trades at about 8.9 times EV/EBITDA and Cheniere at about 11.6, counting Venture Global's preferred and Stonepeak's stake at book and the Cheniere Partners units Cheniere doesn't own at market. At Cheniere's multiple Venture Global would be worth about $22. On trailing EBITDA the gap mostly closes: about 10.7 times for Venture Global, 12.0 for Cheniere and 9.8 for Cheniere Partners. And 2026 is a war year for Venture Global and a contract year for Cheniere. On my 2031 EBITDA of about $7.4 billion, after the wave, today's enterprise value is about 10.6 times. The discount is the war rent rolling off, not a mispricing.

## Scenarios, Options And The Judgment Audit

I weight four cases: bear, the wave lands and the fee is $2.50 (25%); base, $3.50 (45%); bull, replacement cost holds at $4.50 (20%); and management's 2029 framework holding (10%), explained below. At the end of 2028 these are worth $6.29, $11.27, $16.24 and $21.49. The weighted $12.04 is about 5% below today's price including dividends. Equal weights would give $13.82, so the weighting is a call, and I'd rather show it. CP2 timing is the sensitivity most investors don't model: a six-month slip takes today's value to $7.44 and a full year to $5.90.

The options market draws a wider picture. Backing probabilities out of the January 2029 chain, it prices about a 45% chance Venture Global is below $8.75 by then, against 25% on my weights, and about a 24% chance it's above $18.75, against my 10%. The implied median is about $9.80, below my $11.27 base. Option prices carry a premium for protection, so they overstate both tails, and the long-dated chain is thin. Still, my weights aren't aggressive on the downside, and the upside tail deserves respect.

## Path To The Target

The value sits in the 2030s, but three things should reprice it sooner. First, every fee reported on 2029–31 contracts puts a traded price on years the futures only mark. Second, the Q4 deck early next year shows the 2029 contracted share; below 50%, investors see how much is exposed. Third, new capacity starts arriving in 2028, and the 2029–30 strip will reprice as it does. The near-term prints work against the call, which is why the target is two years out.

## Earnings, Quarters And Cash

My 2027 EPS is $2.73 against a $1.00 consensus (S&P Global; Yahoo has the same from nine analysts). One accounting question matters. The 10-Q says commissioning proceeds reduce construction in progress "until assets are placed in service." If CP2's first cargoes are booked that way, 2027 EPS is about $1.91. But at Plaquemines, first LNG came "in December 2024," and in the same month "a portion of the facility's assets, representing $11.4 billion of costs, were placed in service." So revenue is my central reading. To get to $1.00, the Street has to price all 675 TBtu of open 2027 cargoes at about $7.05 against a $15.41 strip, or assume they were sold forward below it. Each $1 on the undisclosed contracted fee moves my 2027 EPS by about $0.47, so that doesn't close the gap either.

The near term isn't where I differ. I get Q3 2026 EPS of $0.54 from the 8-K's volume and fee, and $0.47 for Q4 on the guidance midpoint, against consensus of $0.53 and $0.48 (Yahoo, seven analysts). The gap opens in 2027, at $0.60, $0.42, $0.77 and $0.94 by quarter, with CP2's first cargoes in the second half. So November 9 tests the model's calibration, not the thesis. My call that 2027 EPS beats $1.00 is close to certain on my own numbers, so I don't count it as skill.

Why isn't a stock on about 5 times 2027 earnings cheap? Because the earnings aren't cash. On my numbers Venture Global earns $24.5 billion from 2027 to 2031. After CP2, the bolt-ons, interest, tax and the preferred, about $6.6 billion is left for common shareholders, 19% of the $34.5 billion diluted market value. Free cash flow is -8.1% of market value in 2027 and about 8% to 10% a year from 2029 to 2031, about what a levered, commodity-exposed equity should yield, and it arrives as the curve falls. Earnings drop to $1.12 a share in 2030 and $0.47 in 2031, and net debt is still about $36 billion.

## Balance Sheet And Cash

This is about liquidity, not value. CP2 pays its owner only after both phases are complete and fixed-fee coverage is at least 1.25x. On my split, 71% of 2028's open-cargo margin and 81% of 2029's is commissioning cash inside CP2 and the bolt-ons. In 2029 about $3.1 billion can reach the parent before tax, against about $1.6 billion of parent interest and dividends. The parent's $8.75 billion of notes due from 2029 to 2032 carry a blended 8.9%; with the CP2 bridge due in 2028 and Plaquemines bank debt, about $13.4 billion falls due from 2028 to 2032. Liquidity is fine today: a $2 billion undrawn revolver, a $3 billion 364-day facility from September 2 and $3.1 billion of cash. At June's spreads and today's 10-year, refinancing costs about 7.3%, well below the 8.9% coming due. A wider spread in 2029–32, when the fee is lower, would change that.

## Management's Number

The biggest risk is management's own framework. On the March call, Sabel said a $3 fee on uncontracted volume gives 2029 EBITDA of "about $11 billion." On the August volume outlook and my costs, I get $7.9 billion. About $1.1 billion of the gap is the company's own inconsistency: March implied $3 billion per $1 of fee, against $2.45 billion to $2.50 billion in August's deck. The other $2.0 billion I can't find. If management is right, the stock is worth about $17.90 today and the price needs only $2.32. Hence the 10% weight.

## The Trade

The honest summary is that the edge is in the rating, not in a trade. I can't construct a position with a strong expected return for its risk.

The obvious hedge is short Venture Global against Cheniere. They're the same business on the same coast with different exposure to the 2030s. Each $1 of long-run fee is about 31% of Venture Global's price and 2% to 4% of Cheniere's. Since the war began, Venture Global has run 78% volatility against Cheniere's 33%, with a 0.63 correlation and a beta of 1.51, so the hedge is about $1.50 of Cheniere per $1 short. On my scenarios, with Cheniere earning its CAPM return of about 6.6% a year, the pair returns about +25% weighted over 24 months. But about 20 points of that is simply being long 1.5 times Cheniere's expected return, less under 1 point of borrow. The part that comes from Venture Global is about +5 points over two years, against roughly 85% two-year volatility. I keep the pair on my record as a low-conviction call, and if you run it, size it as a small position.

The options say the same thing. On the morning of October 8 the January 2029 $12.50 put was quoted at $3.30/$3.60 and the $10 put at $2.05/$2.45. The spread costs about $1.20 at the mid and $1.55 if you cross both markets, for a maximum of $2.50. On my scenario weights it's expected to pay about $1.18, roughly fair at the mid and a loser at the offer. The $12.50 put has only 22 contracts of open interest, so treat that leg as illiquid. Borrow was 0.41% on October 7 (IBorrowDesk). With only about 530 million Class A shares trading and 8.4% of that float short, a squeeze is possible in any short.

## Risks To The Rating

J.P. Morgan's case is the strongest outside management's: Overweight with a $17 target since June, on the view that Venture Global can lock in contracts at "outsized" margins. If it signs 2029–31 volume at $4.49 or more, I'm wrong. Qatar first said its damaged Trains 4 and 6 would take three to five years to repair; on September 21 its chief executive said three, which brings them back around 2029. If that slips and the 2030 JKM strip moves from about $10 to above $12, I'm wrong. If the GECF is right that the next tranche needs about $4, the fee could drift there after the wave. Costs could fall to about $0.65, and fees and costs rising together with inflation would lift my value to about $10.70. Bonus depreciation on CP2 would add about $1.06. BP, whose damages hearing is set for May 2027, could get much less than its "$3.7 billion to potentially in excess of $6.0 billion," and two other Calcasieu customers' claims, over $2.4 billion combined, are capped at $425 million in total by Venture Global's reading of the contracts.

None of the 18 analysts S&P Global tracks rates the stock a Sell, and their average target is $16.67. Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan and BofA led the January 2025 IPO. Many of the banks covering the stock, including RBC, Scotiabank, Mizuho and Wells Fargo, also helped arrange the September facility (Morgan Stanley, Citi, UBS and Bernstein did not), and Goldman and J.P. Morgan sit in CP2's lender group.

Catalysts: Cheniere's results October 29, Phase 1 COD around October 31, Venture Global's Q3 results November 9, the Q4 deck early next year, the BP damages hearing in May 2027 and CP2 first LNG in the second half of 2027.

## So

Sell, with a 24-month target of $11.25 and moderate conviction. The rating carries the view. If you trade it, the Cheniere pair or the put spread is a small, low-edge way to express it.
